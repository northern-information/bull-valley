# Multiplayer

One Cloudflare Worker serves the bundle and routes `/ws` to one Durable Object, `ValleyDO`, named `bull-valley`: everyone online is in the same valley and shares one persistent world. Every change to the valley runs under one lock in `ValleyDO` (`locked`): the reducer runs and its result is applied with nothing between, D1 writes included, so the shadowmen's step never lands between a handler's reduce and its apply (`blockConcurrencyWhile` holds inbound events, not the step's timer, so only the constructor's storage read uses it); what is one account's alone (the season's and the daily task's tallies, the Book of Shadows) runs under that account's own lock (`forAccount`). State frames and chat touch no valley and never wait on either. Each socket's player lives in its attachment (WebSocket Hibernation API); the world lives in the object's storage, and a storage alarm wakes it for Marx's next change and the day's turn while anyone is online. The client sends a state frame (`x y z yaw pitch pose riding light held?`, light being the flashlight up and on, held the jar or the rod in the hand in its place, or `none` for anything out of sight (the phone, the whistle, a pack), missing while it holds the flashlight; `held.ts`) at most `CONFIG.net.sendHz` a second and only when it changed; peers are drawn `CONFIG.net.interpolateMs` behind the present, between the two of their last few seconds of frames either side of that moment (`presence.ts`; a move after a silence starts a frame before it, and our own reconnect keeps what we had of everyone). The valley stamps each `peer-state` it passes on with its clock (`t`), and a client keeps the frame as having come when that stamp was due here, half a round trip on (`stampOf`), never later than it came nor before the frame ahead: frames a stalled line lets through together keep the spacing they were sent with, rather than all landing at once and jumping. A raider whose socket goes stands where they were for `CONFIG.net.lingerMs` before everyone hears them leave (`ValleyDO` `linger`); a reconnect from the same account that names its old id (`was`) inside it takes that id and place back, and no one hears them go or come, while a fresh arrival on the account (a reload) lets them go at once. The client lets go of a line whose ping goes unanswered past `CONFIG.net.deadMs` and reconnects, and sends where it stands after every welcome. Same origin, no CORS. Nothing plays without the valley: Begin waits for its welcome (the intro overlay says Reaching the Valley…), and a drop stops the raider with the pointer free and the overlay saying Reconnecting… while `net.ts` reconnects; after `CONFIG.net.giveUpMs` without the valley the overlay offers the way back to the menu (`valleysync.ts`, `hud.ts` `setConnection`). The client keeps no valley of its own: it draws what the valley sends.

Chat is one channel for the whole valley, live only: Enter opens the field in the lower left (every key types into it until Enter sends, or Esc frees the pointer to the menu and drops the draft), a `chat` frame carries the line, and the valley sends it back to everyone, the sender included, under the name it holds and with its own timestamp. Nothing is stored. A line starting with `/` is a command answered in the typer's own log and never sent (`chat.ts` `chatCommand`): `/online` names everyone in the valley, each with their level, `/emotes` lists the emotes, and an emote's own name takes it: `/wave`, `/sit`, `/smoke`, `/dance`, `/point`, `/shrug`, `/kneel` (`emotes.ts`). An emote is a pose (`poses.ts`) the raider's body takes, carried to everyone by the state frame's `pose` field (`protocol.ts` `PEER_POSES`, named as typed), so nothing else goes on the wire; a raider within `CONFIG.emotes.seenRadius` of one beginning gets a quiet line in the log (`seenEmote`, `chat.ts` `emoteLine`), and the emoter's own log says what they did. Each holds for its seconds (`/sit`, `/kneel` and `/dance` until the raider moves), and moving, crouching, boarding or a strike ends it; sitting and kneeling lower the eye. Never from the bed. Friends and whispers (rule 21, `friends.ts`) are commands too, sent to the valley rather than said: `/w name message` (or `/whisper`) goes to that raider's sockets alone and back to the sender's, inked pink, under the chat rules and the chat rate, never stored; `/friend name` asks a raider to be friends (or accepts their asking: the two are friends once both have asked), `/unfriend name` ends it or takes a request back, and `/friends` lists them in the log, each friend in the valley with roughly where (riding with Marx, in the corn maze, by a Citgo, out in the valley, from their last state frame). A friend coming into the valley says so in their friends' logs. Friendships are the accounts', in D1 (`friends`, one row each way; `AccountStore.askFriend`, `friendsOf`, `unfriend`), and only usernames go on the wire (`whisper`, `friend`, `unfriend` and `friends` frames up; `whisper`, `friends` and `friend-news` down). An `inspect` frame names a raider by username, in any case; the valley finds the account (`accountByUsername`), reads its XP, look, cosmetics, Book of Shadows, season progress and the asker's friends list from D1, and answers the asker's socket alone with an `inspected` frame carrying the card (`inspect.ts` `cardOf`, `InspectWire`: no account id), or a nack (`unknown`, `too-fast` past ten in ten seconds, `unavailable`). Parties (sharedworld rule 34, `party.ts`) are the valley's alone, kept in `Valley.parties` by account and never in D1: `invite {name}` (the friend rate) reaches only a raider in the valley and either makes an invite or, inviting back, joins; `party-leave` leaves; each change sends every member whose party changed a `party` frame (`{members: {name, online}[], invites}`, also sent on hello when there is anything in it) and `party-news` (`invited`, `sent`, `joined`, `left`). A raider whose last socket closes is marked away and stays a member through `CONFIG.net.lingerMs`; the linger's timer reduces `party-drop`, and any arrival also drops a member away longer than that (so a valley woken without its timers lets go too). `party-chat {text}` goes to every member's sockets under the chat rules and rate, and is never kept. The Caretaker's burning credits every party member placed within `CONFIG.party.creditRadius` of a burner (`creditedWithParty`). Crews (sharedworld rule 35, `crews.ts`) are kept in D1 (`migrations/0018_crews.sql`: `crews` with unique `tag` and `name` in any case, `crew_members` one row an account, `crew_invites`) through `ValleyDO` `crews()` (`worker/d1crews.ts`). `crew-create {tag, name}` writes the crew, the leader's membership and `CONFIG.crews.cost` out of the wallet in one batch (the wallet's CHECK rolls a short one back), then sends the pack frame; `crew-invite {name}`, `crew-join {tag}`, `crew-leave` and `crew-kick {name}` go under the valley's lock at the friend rate, each answered with `crew` (`{crew: {name, tag, leader, members}, invites}`) to every account it touched and `crew-news`, or a nack. A member's tag rides `PeerWire.crew`, read at hello and changed for everyone with `peer-updated` the moment it changes (`recrew`). `crew-chat {text}` goes to every member's sockets under the chat rules and rate, and is never kept. A name no raider could have is refused in the client, since the valley closes a malformed frame. Beginning to play says how many others are in the valley, and a raider joining says their name. The text rules (`normalizeChat`, `CHAT_MAX`) are `protocol.ts`'s, and the valley nacks past 5 lines in 10 seconds. Every line in the log carries its local `HH:MM` (the valley's timestamp for a raider's line) and is inked by who said it: raiders blue, NPCs gold, the game's own lines eggshell. Older lines thin out toward the top. The log scrolls with the wheel or PageUp/PageDown (`bindings.ts` `CHAT`) while the field is open, and on hover while the pointer is free (the pack or the book open); paused, it lies under the overlay like the rest of the HUD.

Sign-in is required. The Worker's `/auth` routes (`worker/auth.ts`) sign raiders in with Google, Discord or GitHub (OAuth with PKCE; a Dev provider on dev servers only), or with a Coach key, and keep the session in httpOnly cookies: a 15-minute access token, a 30-day refresh token that carries the account's session number (`accounts.session`) and is reissued on every use; signing out moves the number on (`AccountStore.endSessions`), so every refresh token the account had, on any browser, is dead at once, and the access tokens within the quarter hour. The game is private: a new raider is held as a pending signup until they say the magic word (a secret of the Worker's, `env.ts` `magicWord`, checked there alone and never shipped to the client; a wrong one keeps the signup pending), then chooses a username (3 to 16 letters, digits or underscores, unique without regard to case). A Coach account has no provider behind it: `POST /auth/coach/create` makes a key of 256 random hex characters (`oauth.ts` `newCoachKey`), answers it in the body that once, and holds its SHA-256 as the pending signup's identity (`coach:<digest>` in `providers`; the key itself is never stored, logged or set in a cookie), so the magic word and the username follow as for anyone. `POST /auth/coach/login` signs in by the key alone (pasted spaces and case forgiven, `account.ts` `coachKeyOf`); a key no account has is told so and nothing more, since there is no email, no reset and no recovery. `POST /auth/link/coach` puts one key on an account signed in another way, after which the other providers can be unlinked and the key is the only way in. All three are on the strict rate limit. Your Name, in Settings (the main menu's, the pause overlay's, Dial-Up's lobby), changes it any time (`PUT /auth/username`), and Moab Coldë changes the raider's character (Your Character, a tab beside his trade); a `rename` frame then has the valley read the new username from D1 (never from the client), an `appearance` frame carries the new outfit, and everyone gets a `peer-updated` frame for every socket the account has signed in (the 3D valley and Dial-Up both), the asker's last. Each app takes on its own name and body from its own `peer-updated`, and says nothing of a peer under its own name: that is the account on its other app, whose news it has said already. Accounts live in D1 (`bull-valley-accounts`, `migrations/`), and so does what each account looks like: the character and guitar finish chosen at the select or at Moab are saved with `PUT /auth/look` and come back in `/auth/me`, so they follow the raider to any browser, and `/auth/me` carries the account's cosmetics too (`AccountStore.cosmeticsOf`), so the select dresses every character in them before the valley answers; the hotbar is kept the same way (`PUT /auth/hotbar`, the `accounts.hotbar` column), and so are the settings, the music and sound volumes (`PUT /auth/settings`, the `accounts.settings` column, saved a moment after a slider is let go). Nothing the game keeps lives in the browser's storage. The Worker reads the access cookie on the `/ws` upgrade and stamps the account and username on the request it forwards; the client never sends a name, and a `hello` with no stamped account is closed `unauthenticated` (4007), which sends the player back to sign in rather than into the valley alone. The client refreshes the session before every socket open, and every route that needs a session (`/auth/me` and the saves: username, look, hotbar, settings, link, unlink) slides a lapsed access cookie on the refresh cookie as it answers, since the socket outlives the quarter hour. `/auth/me` always answers 200, and so does `/auth/refresh` unless it is rate limited, so a signed-out visitor is not an error.

The shared world's rules are `src/sharedworld.ts`, a pure reducer the Worker runs and `tests/unit/sharedworld.test.ts` pins. Every action first settles what was due by now (the day's turn, Marx's next changes), then acts. The world is persistent (rule 1): the first build to arrive opens it, it is kept in storage whoever comes and goes, and only a build on a newer protocol (`SharedWorld.version`) opens it again. That build's hello brings the places (pickups, stations, forecourts, the maze, the stand, the recycling machines, the rings, the routes); what the older world kept is put by as the valley is restored (`restoreValley`, `Valley.carried`, read field by field so a bad or missing field is left behind) and laid into the new world on the next join (`carryOver`): the tombstones always, the drops (a kind this build knows) and the shelves' units only while their day is still today, the shelves only when the station count matches (each row checked against the build's layout); the pickups taken, the truck, the portals and Erwin's stock start afresh, since they index the old placements or come back with the day. A `reset` drops what was carried too. Where each account last stood on foot is kept (`Valley.places`, rule 2) and comes back in the welcome (`place`), which puts the raider there. It is kept as the raider walks, not only when the socket closes: a state frame keeps it (the `stand` action) at most every `CONFIG.net.placeEveryMs` once they have moved `placeMoved` metres, and a hello first keeps where the account's other open sockets stand, so a tab that vanished without closing, or a reload whose hello beats the old tab's close, comes back near where it was. Matthew Marx's truck is the valley's (rule 3): a chain of legs stamped with server times (`marx.ts`: parked with Marx reading, donuts, the joyride, called to a whistler, the ferry home), each moved on by `settleTruck` when it is due; the valley never knows the roads, so the hello tells it where the truck parks and how long the joyride takes (`TruckRoutes`), and it reckons any other drive generously. Every client drives the same leg against the same clock (`truckplan.ts` `planLeg`, `Truck.follow`). A `board` frame puts a raider in the bed (the countdown starts with the first), a `charter` frame from the first one in names where he goes (a point inside the survey, or null for the joyride; `marx.ts` `charter`, which with no one else in the valley leaves at once, and a `charter` leg that lets the bed off there with a `dropoff` snapshot), `hop-out` takes them out, and `call` whistles (rule 5: an empty truck only, one at a time; boarding the truck you whistled is the ride home). Pickups are shared by index into `world.pickups`, first to ask wins (rule 4), and every pickup, a cabbage included, goes into the pack, but cash (the plaza's quarters, the $20 in its office, `drops.ts` `QUARTERS`, `isCash`), which pays the wallet (`Reduced.earn`). Every take, buy, berry, loot, board and whistle is held to where the raider's last state frame put them (`CONFIG.valleyReach`, `sharedworld.ts` `tooFar`: `no-position` with no frame heard, `too-far` past it), never to where a frame says they are; a whistle comes to that spot, and the drive is reckoned from where the client says the truck is unless that is further than `callFrom` from home. The day turns at midnight Central (rule 6, `daily.ts`): the pickups come back, the shelves fill, the drops go. The shadowmen are the valley's too (rule 11): while anyone is placed in the valley it steps one field `CONFIG.shadowmen.tickHz` times a second (`stepShadows`, a timer that keeps the object awake until no one is left), crossing the bubble round every raider, and sends each socket the shadowmen within `despawnRadius` (and a margin) of its own raider in a `shadowmen` frame each step (`shadowmenNear`; a raider nowhere yet gets the whole field); clients draw it a beat behind the present (`shadowsync.ts`). It rushes only a raider on foot and out of the bed, winds up before it lunges (`Shadowman.windup`, sent as `windup`, and the ids that lunged in the frame's `lunges`, so every client draws the strike), a touch sends that raider alone a `struck` frame carrying the health left (rule 24), and it burns in any raider's beam, aimed from their state frame (`shadowmen.ts` `beamFrom`) and only while that frame says the flashlight is in hand (no `held`, `shadowstep.ts` `shadowRaiders`), and in Marx's headlights (`headlightBeam`), which credit no one: the valley never knows the roads, so a client within `CONFIG.shadowmen.despawnRadius` of the truck sends where it stands (`Truck.headlights`) in a `headlights` frame on the state frame's cadence, and the valley aims them from the last word of a raider within reach of it while that is fresh (`shadowstep.ts` `seeHeadlights`, `headlightsAt`). Each burst leaves a drop of dimes (`drops.ts` `DIMES`, how many drawn by the valley with `dimesFor` and spilled with a `spill` action, a `spilled` snapshot); a `take-drop` of dimes pays the wallet (`Reduced.earn`, `PackStore.earn`) instead of the pack. Each shadowman's burst also leaves a tombstone (rule 17; no other kind does): the valley names it with `graves.ts` `burialsOf` and passes the burials in the same `spill` action, and `WorldWire.graves` carries every one; they outlast the day's turn. A shadow spider is one of the same field (`Shadowman.kind`, sent as `kind: 'spider'`, its burn against its own longer time): each new arrival in a bubble is a spider by a small chance, or a far larger one where it comes in, or its raider stands, near one of its lairs (`lairs.ts`: the dish array, Castle Entropy, Bull Valley Plaza and the industrial park, which the hello places as `lairs`, kept in the world), never more than a few round one raider (`shadowmen.ts` `spiderComes`), and its burst spills one $20 bill (`drops.ts` `TWENTY`, `centsOf`) that pays the wallet like the dimes. The hello carries each station's place and the survey's size for it, all kept in the world. It carries the lurks too (`lurks`, `industrial.ts` `parkLurks`: three in each of the industrial park's buildings), kept in the world: at each the step forms a lurker, a shadowman that keeps to it (`shadowmen.ts` `formLurkers`), once a raider is within `CONFIG.shadowmen.lurk.wake` and none within `clear`, drifts it round the spot and lets it rush like any other; it is no raider's bubble, is dropped past every bubble like the rest, and one burst waits `respawnSeconds` (`ShadowmenField.lurkWaits`) before it forms again. The Caretaker is the valley's the same way (rule 13): stepped beside the shadowmen and sent in the same frame (`caretaker`, and `burned` where two beams burned it), in the maze the hello placed (`MazePlace`, kept in the world); its touch sends a `struck` frame with `by: 'caretaker'`, and it hunts only a raider a shadowman could rush. Where it is burned the valley spills two drops of gold bullion, a one troy ounce bar each (`drops.ts` `spillsOf`, the same `spill` action as the dimes, which names each drop's kind), into the taker's pack. Corpse runs are the valley's (rule 18, `corpses.ts`): a strike's `struck` frame is followed by a fall, alone in the valley (`ValleyDO` `fall`): the account's pack is emptied in one D1 batch (`PackStore.strip`, every kind left at zero but the key items, which stay in the pack), the wallet is read and the reducer's `spend` for the strike's share of it (`corpses.ts` `spilledDimes`) is taken out of it (`PackStore.purchase` with no item; if that fails, nothing spills) and left lying as a spilled drop of dimes where the raider fell, and the reducer lays a body (`Valley.corpses`, with what it holds and the account, never on the wire) where the raider's last state frame put them, one an account (a second fall takes the first body away, and what it still held is lost, whether or not it lays a new one), or the valley gives it all back (`PackStore.give`). `WorldWire.corpses` carries every body without its contents (`CorpseWire`), and the welcome and every `pack` frame carry the ids of the account's own (`corpses`). A `loot` frame names a body; only its account may, and the things go back into the pack before the body is gone (`fell` and `looted` snapshots). A key item never lies on a body: one left on a body laid before strikes kept them goes back into its account's pack as the account comes into the valley (the reducer's `keys-back`, `corpses.ts` `keysOff`, `ValleyDO` `keysBack`, giving the pack the keys before the body loses them), and a body left with nothing goes (a `looted` snapshot). The bodies are the valley's, not the world's, so neither the day's turn nor a world opened afresh takes them. The Recycling Center's research is the account's (rule 33, `recycling.ts`, D1 `research`, one row an account and line): the hello says where each Citgo's recycling machine stands (`recyclers`, one a station, kept in the world), a `research` frame answers with a `research` frame of the levels, and a `recycle` frame buys a line's next level only when the raider's last state frame put them within `CONFIG.recycling.tendReach` of any of them (dialed in, from anywhere), the level and the empties out of the pack written in one batch; every socket on the account hears the `research` frame with its `line`, then the pack. The campfire (rule 40, `cooking.ts`) keeps nothing of its own: a `cook` frame (a raw fish and a count) cooks only when the raider's last state frame put them within `CONFIG.fishing.campfire.tendReach` of a pond's campfire, where `campfireOf` puts it off the pond the hello already sends (dialed in, from anywhere), and is written as a barter with Erwin is, the fish out and what they cook into in, in one batch under the valley's lock (`PackStore.barter`); every socket on the account hears a `cooked` frame, then the pack. Research never touches the shared world: loose change reads the taker's research as a `take-drop` lands, and only over dimes a burst left (`Drop.burst`, never a spilled wallet) adds as many again, on the valley's roll, to the taker's own wallet, with a `found` frame to the account; the drop and the `drop-taken` everyone sees are as they were. The kit is the account's (rule 38): every account carries the flashlight and the Citgo phone from its first welcome (`items.ts` `start`), and the `discover` that first writes `marx` in its book gives it Marx's whistle (`book.ts` `GIFTS`, `sharedworld.ts` `giftsOwed`), as does the welcome of an account that met him before the whistle was a thing; the pack frame that follows says so. A `call` and a `portal` read the pack first (`ValleyDO` `withPack`) and are nacked `no-whistle` or `no-phone` without it. Fishing is the account's (rule 36, `fishing.ts`): the hello says where the firefly ponds lie (`ponds`, kept in the world), and `stepShadows` turns every surface shadow aside at each as at a forecourt (`shadeHavens`, each haven with its own `radius`). The valley keeps each account's angler (`Valley.anglers`: the skill in tenths, today's count, when it may dig) in its own storage, and each socket's line out (`Valley.lines`, gone with the socket). An `angler` frame answers with an `angler` frame; `tackle` (a rod when the pack has none), `dig` and `cast` stand only when the raider's last state frame put them inside the pond's haven (dialed in, from anywhere), `tackle` and `cast` with the pack read first under the lock. A cast fishes with the best rod in the pack (`rodIn`), and the line keeps it, so the fight and the catch are reckoned from the skill and that rod together (`reachOf`); the rods on each Citgo's rack are shelf units like any other (rule 7). A `jar-slurm` frame names one slurm of one drop (station, slot, index); the valley reads the pack (a jar with room), checks the raider stands at that Citgo, that the drop could still be crawling (`slurms.ts` `mayJar`, from the same seed every client draws it from) and that no one jarred it first (`Valley.jarred`, kept only for recent slots), puts the slurm in the pack and sends everyone a `slurm-jarred` frame, which takes it off every client's lot. The topiaries stand where the valley's clock has them, with nothing on the wire, but the valley keeps each one's health (sharedworld rule 39, `SharedWorld.topiaries`, on every world snapshot as `topiaries`): a `toolbox` frame from a raider on foot by the toolbox at the lookout's foot (`lookout.ts` `toolboxOf`) puts a torch in a pack that holds none, and a `torch` frame names the topiary a swing landed on (its index in `TOPIARIES`); the valley reads the pack (a torch), checks the raider's last state frame put them within reach of the spine of one of that topiary's own spots (`topiaries.ts` `inTorchReach`) and that their last swing was a swing ago (`SharedWorld.swings`, never on the wire), takes `torchDamage` off it on its own roll, and sends everyone a world frame, `topiary-hit` or, at nothing, `topiary-burned` (with `index` and `damage`); a burned one grows back whole on a `topiary-back` frame, the valley waking for it as it does for the truck. Both frames need a body. A moat slurm goes by station `MOAT_STATION` (-1, `moatslurms.ts`): the valley checks instead that the raider stands in the channel (`moat.ts` `inChannel`, the moat the world was opened with) and that the drop could still lie on the floor (`moatslurms.ts` `mayJarMoat`, from the drop's seed alone, no sources needed), and keeps those keys by the moat's own short slots. A `cast` draws what bites on the valley's roll and answers the caster alone with a `line` frame (`bitesIn`, and the hook's call, feeling and fight with the seed its arrows come from, never the fish), the bait out of the pack when something bit; a `reel` is judged against the fight both sides reckon (`reelInTime`), and answers with a `catch` frame, the fish into the pack and the `angler` frame to every socket on the account. The Cabbage Stand is the account's (rule 23, `stand.ts`, D1 `stands`): the hello says where it stands (kept in the world), a `stand-stock`, `stand-collect` or `stand-upgrade` frame tends it only when the raider's last state frame put them within `CONFIG.stand.tendReach` of it, and the valley, alone (under its lock), reads the ledger, the pack and the wallet, judges the change, and writes all three in one transaction (`PackStore.tend`, guarded by the ledger's write count); a `stand` frame (the ledger, and the cents for a collect) goes to every socket on the account with a `pack` frame after, and the welcome carries the ledger. Erwin von Dutch is the valley's (rule 25, `dealer.ts`): the hello says where he sits (kept in the world), his stock today rides in `WorldWire.dealer` (full again when the day turns), and a `deal` frame names a good and the offer that pays for it (kind to units, 1 to 16 kinds), only when the raider's last state frame put them within `CONFIG.dealer.dealReach` of him; the valley, alone (under its lock), reads the pack, judges the barter (`deal`: a good he has left, an offer of only what a shelf prices and the pack holds, enough of it at `CONFIG.dealer.rate`, and no unit to spare, `over` otherwise, since he makes no change), and writes every side in one transaction (`PackStore.barter`, the whole offer out under one guard or none of it); everyone gets a `dealt` snapshot, the account a `pack` frame. No cash changes hands. Quests are the account's (rule 26, `quests.ts`, D1 `quests`): a `quest` frame names the quest and the step (`accept` beside Erwin, `lay` at the heart of the maze the world was opened with, `reward` beside Erwin), the valley, alone (under its lock), reads the stage and the pack, judges the step (`questStep`), and writes the stage and the pack change together (`PackStore.quest`, guarded by the stage it found); a `quest` frame (the stage) goes to every socket on the account with a `pack` frame after, and the welcome carries every quest's stage. The Undercroft is the valley's too (rule 11, `undercroft.ts`, `tunnelshades.ts`): the ground is a heightfield, so it is a sealed stone box raised `CONFIG.undercroft.lift` over a far corner of the survey square (`undercroftPlace`), its floor a registered floor and its stone walls on `walls`, so no one on the surface ever walks in; the trapdoor and the ladder move a raider there and back (client-only, `player.relocate`). The hello says where it lies (kept in the world); `stepShadows` keeps a raider down there out of the shadowmen's field and the Caretaker's reach, and steps the tunnel shades on its grid with the Caretaker's own pathing (`stepTunnelShades`), sending them in the same `shadowmen` frame (`tunnel`, each with `hurt`, the share of its hits taken; `tunnelBursts`, and `tunnelWounds` where the Warden took a hit it stands); a touch sends a `struck` frame with `by: 'tunnel'`, and each burst spills dimes like a shadowman's (but no tombstone) and counts as a burn for the daily task (the Warden earns its own XP, `warden`). The lookout tower by the maze's gate is out of every shadow's reach (rule 11, `lookout.ts`): it stands in the maze's frame, so the valley finds it from the `MazePlace` the hello already carries, and a raider whose last state frame puts them over its walled footprint is up on the deck, never `vulnerable` (`shadowRaiders`, `onLookout`), their beam still aimed; its deck is a registered floor and its ladder moves a raider up and down (client-only, `player.relocate`). The shadowmen have heights. Once a wake the valley reads the map from the Worker's own assets (`env.ASSETS`, `heightmap.ts` `readHeightGrid`: terrain.png decoded without a canvas, to the same grid the client stands on) and cuts in Castle Entropy's moat where the hello says it lies (`moat`, a `MazePlace`; `valleyGround`). Each shadow stands at the ground's height under it (`y` on `ShadowmanWire` and on a `Burst`) and reaches only feet within `CONFIG.shadowmen.vertical` of it (`shadowmen.ts` `reachesUp`, against the `y` of the raider's last state frame): it never rushes nor strikes a raider up a fire escape over it, or on the rim over the moat's floor, or the other way round, and a beam finds it where it stands, so a raider on the rim burns what walks the floor.
