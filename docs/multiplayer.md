# Multiplayer

One Cloudflare Worker serves the bundle and routes `/ws` to one Durable Object, `ValleyDO`, named `bull-valley`: everyone online is in the same valley and shares one persistent world. Every change to the valley runs under one lock in `ValleyDO` (`locked`): the reducer runs and its result is applied with nothing between, D1 writes included, so the shadowmen's step never lands between a handler's reduce and its apply (`blockConcurrencyWhile` holds inbound events, not the step's timer, so only the constructor's storage read uses it); what is one account's alone (the season's and the daily task's tallies, the Book of Shadows) runs under that account's own lock (`forAccount`). State frames and chat touch no valley and never wait on either. Each socket's player lives in its attachment (WebSocket Hibernation API); the world lives in the object's storage, and a storage alarm wakes it for Marx's next change and the day's turn while anyone is online. The client sends a state frame (`x y z yaw pitch pose riding light`, light being the flashlight up and on) at most `CONFIG.net.sendHz` a second and only when it changed; peers are drawn `CONFIG.net.interpolateMs` behind the present, between the two of their last few seconds of frames either side of that moment (`presence.ts`; a move after a silence starts a frame before it, and our own reconnect keeps what we had of everyone). The valley stamps each `peer-state` it passes on with its clock (`t`), and a client keeps the frame as having come when that stamp was due here, half a round trip on (`stampOf`), never later than it came nor before the frame ahead: frames a stalled line lets through together keep the spacing they were sent with, rather than all landing at once and jumping. A raider whose socket goes stands where they were for `CONFIG.net.lingerMs` before everyone hears them leave (`ValleyDO` `linger`); a reconnect from the same account that names its old id (`was`) inside it takes that id and place back, and no one hears them go or come, while a fresh arrival on the account (a reload) lets them go at once. The client lets go of a line whose ping goes unanswered past `CONFIG.net.deadMs` and reconnects, and sends where it stands after every welcome. Same origin, no CORS. Nothing plays without the valley: Begin waits for its welcome (the intro overlay says Reaching the Valley…), and a drop stops the raider with the pointer free and the overlay saying Reconnecting… while `net.ts` reconnects; after `CONFIG.net.giveUpMs` without the valley the overlay offers the way back to the menu (`valleysync.ts`, `hud.ts` `setConnection`). The client keeps no valley of its own: it draws what the valley sends.

Chat is one channel for the whole valley, live only: Enter opens the field in the lower left (every key types into it until Enter sends or Esc pauses), a `chat` frame carries the line, and the valley sends it back to everyone, the sender included, under the name it holds and with its own timestamp. Nothing is stored. A line starting with `/` is a command answered in the typer's own log and never sent (`chat.ts` `chatCommand`): `/online` names everyone in the valley, each with their level, `/emotes` lists the emotes, and an emote's own name takes it: `/wave`, `/sit`, `/smoke`, `/dance`, `/point`, `/shrug`, `/kneel` (`emotes.ts`). An emote is a pose (`poses.ts`) the raider's body takes, carried to everyone by the state frame's `pose` field (`protocol.ts` `PEER_POSES`, named as typed), so nothing else goes on the wire; a raider within `CONFIG.emotes.seenRadius` of one beginning gets a quiet line in the log (`seenEmote`, `chat.ts` `emoteLine`), and the emoter's own log says what they did. Each holds for its seconds (`/sit`, `/kneel` and `/dance` until the raider moves), and moving, crouching, boarding or a strike ends it; sitting and kneeling lower the eye. Never from the bed. Friends and whispers (rule 21, `friends.ts`) are commands too, sent to the valley rather than said: `/w name message` (or `/whisper`) goes to that raider's sockets alone and back to the sender's, inked pink, under the chat rules and the chat rate, never stored; `/friend name` asks a raider to be friends (or accepts their asking: the two are friends once both have asked), `/unfriend name` ends it or takes a request back, and `/friends` lists them in the log, each friend in the valley with roughly where (riding with Marx, in the corn maze, by a Citgo, out in the valley, from their last state frame). A friend coming into the valley says so in their friends' logs. Friendships are the accounts', in D1 (`friends`, one row each way; `AccountStore.askFriend`, `friendsOf`, `unfriend`), and only usernames go on the wire (`whisper`, `friend`, `unfriend` and `friends` frames up; `whisper`, `friends` and `friend-news` down). An `inspect` frame names a raider by username, in any case; the valley finds the account (`accountByUsername`), reads its XP, look, cosmetics, Book of Shadows, season progress and the asker's friends list from D1, and answers the asker's socket alone with an `inspected` frame carrying the card (`inspect.ts` `cardOf`, `InspectWire`: no account id), or a nack (`unknown`, `too-fast` past ten in ten seconds, `unavailable`). A name no raider could have is refused in the client, since the valley closes a malformed frame. Beginning to play says how many others are in the valley, and a raider joining says their name. The text rules (`normalizeChat`, `CHAT_MAX`) are `protocol.ts`'s, and the valley nacks past 5 lines in 10 seconds. Every line in the log carries its local `HH:MM` (the valley's timestamp for a raider's line) and is inked by who said it: raiders blue, NPCs gold, the game's own lines eggshell. Older lines thin out toward the top. The log scrolls with the wheel or PageUp/PageDown (`bindings.ts` `CHAT`) while the field is open, and on hover while the pointer is free (the pack or the book open); paused, it lies under the overlay like the rest of the HUD.

Sign-in is required. The Worker's `/auth` routes (`worker/auth.ts`) sign raiders in with Google, Discord or GitHub (OAuth with PKCE; a Dev provider on dev servers only) and keep the session in httpOnly cookies: a 15-minute access token, a 30-day refresh token that carries the account's session number (`accounts.session`) and is reissued on every use; signing out moves the number on (`AccountStore.endSessions`), so every refresh token the account had, on any browser, is dead at once, and the access tokens within the quarter hour. The game is private: a new raider is held as a pending signup until they say the magic word (a secret of the Worker's, `env.ts` `magicWord`, checked there alone and never shipped to the client; a wrong one keeps the signup pending), then chooses a username (3 to 16 letters, digits or underscores, unique without regard to case). Gron, the hunched NPC beside the berry bush, changes it any time (`PUT /auth/username`) and changes the raider's character too; a `rename` frame then has the valley read the new username from D1 (never from the client), an `appearance` frame carries the new outfit, and everyone gets a `peer-updated` frame for every socket the account has signed in (the 3D valley and Dial-Up both), the asker's last. Each app takes on its own name and body from its own `peer-updated`, and says nothing of a peer under its own name: that is the account on its other app, whose news it has said already. Accounts live in D1 (`bull-valley-accounts`, `migrations/`), and so does what each account looks like: the character and guitar finish chosen at the select or at Gron are saved with `PUT /auth/look` and come back in `/auth/me`, so they follow the raider to any browser, and `/auth/me` carries the account's cosmetics too (`AccountStore.cosmeticsOf`), so the select dresses every character in them before the valley answers; the hotbar is kept the same way (`PUT /auth/hotbar`, the `accounts.hotbar` column), and so are the settings, the music and sound volumes (`PUT /auth/settings`, the `accounts.settings` column, saved a moment after a slider is let go). Nothing the game keeps lives in the browser's storage. The Worker reads the access cookie on the `/ws` upgrade and stamps the account and username on the request it forwards; the client never sends a name, and a `hello` with no stamped account is closed `unauthenticated` (4007), which sends the player back to sign in rather than into the valley alone. The client refreshes the session before every socket open. `/auth/me` always answers 200, and so does `/auth/refresh` unless it is rate limited, so a signed-out visitor is not an error.

The shared world's rules are `src/sharedworld.ts`, a pure reducer the Worker runs and `tests/unit/sharedworld.test.ts` pins. Every action first settles what was due by now (the day's turn, Marx's next changes), then acts. The world is persistent (rule 1): the first build to arrive opens it, it is kept in storage whoever comes and goes, and only a build on a newer protocol (`SharedWorld.version`) opens it afresh. Where each account last stood on foot is kept (`Valley.places`, rule 2) and comes back in the welcome (`place`), which puts the raider there. Matthew Marx's truck is the valley's (rule 3): a chain of legs stamped with server times (`marx.ts`: parked with Marx reading, donuts, the joyride, called to a whistler, the ferry home), each moved on by `settleTruck` when it is due; the valley never knows the roads, so the hello tells it where the truck parks and how long the joyride takes (`TruckRoutes`), and it reckons any other drive generously. Every client drives the same leg against the same clock (`truckplan.ts` `planLeg`, `Truck.follow`). A `board` frame puts a raider in the bed (the countdown starts with the first), a `charter` frame from the first one in names where he goes (a point inside the survey, or null for the joyride; `marx.ts` `charter`, which with no one else in the valley leaves at once, and a `charter` leg that lets the bed off there with a `dropoff` snapshot), `hop-out` takes them out, and `call` whistles (rule 5: an empty truck only, one at a time; boarding the truck you whistled is the ride home). Pickups are shared by index into `world.pickups`, first to ask wins (rule 4), and every pickup, a cabbage included, goes into the pack, but cash (the plaza's quarters, the $20 in its office, `drops.ts` `QUARTERS`, `isCash`), which pays the wallet (`Reduced.earn`). Every take, buy, berry, loot, board and whistle is held to where the raider's last state frame put them (`CONFIG.valleyReach`, `sharedworld.ts` `tooFar`: `no-position` with no frame heard, `too-far` past it), never to where a frame says they are; a whistle comes to that spot, and the drive is reckoned from where the client says the truck is unless that is further than `callFrom` from home. The day turns at midnight Central (rule 6, `daily.ts`): the pickups come back, the shelves fill, the drops go. The shadowmen are the valley's too (rule 11): while anyone is placed in the valley it steps one field `CONFIG.shadowmen.tickHz` times a second (`stepShadows`, a timer that keeps the object awake until no one is left), crossing the bubble round every raider, and sends each socket the shadowmen within `despawnRadius` (and a margin) of its own raider in a `shadowmen` frame each step (`shadowmenNear`; a raider nowhere yet gets the whole field); clients draw it a beat behind the present (`shadowsync.ts`). It rushes only a raider on foot and out of the bed, winds up before it lunges (`Shadowman.windup`, sent as `windup`, and the ids that lunged in the frame's `lunges`, so every client draws the strike), a touch sends that raider alone a `struck` frame carrying the health left (rule 24), and it burns in any raider's beam, aimed from their state frame (`shadowmen.ts` `beamFrom`), and in Marx's headlights (`headlightBeam`), which credit no one: the valley never knows the roads, so a client within `CONFIG.shadowmen.despawnRadius` of the truck sends where it stands (`Truck.headlights`) in a `headlights` frame on the state frame's cadence, and the valley aims them from the last word of a raider within reach of it while that is fresh (`shadowstep.ts` `seeHeadlights`, `headlightsAt`). Each burst leaves a drop of dimes (`drops.ts` `DIMES`, how many drawn by the valley with `dimesFor` and spilled with a `spill` action, a `spilled` snapshot); a `take-drop` of dimes pays the wallet (`Reduced.earn`, `PackStore.earn`) instead of the pack. Each shadowman's burst also leaves a tombstone (rule 17; no other kind does): the valley names it with `graves.ts` `burialsOf` and passes the burials in the same `spill` action, and `WorldWire.graves` carries every one; they outlast the day's turn. A shadow spider is one of the same field (`Shadowman.kind`, sent as `kind: 'spider'`, its burn against its own longer time): each new arrival in a bubble is a spider by a small chance, or a far larger one where it comes in, or its raider stands, near water, never more than a few round one raider (`shadowmen.ts` `spiderComes`), and its burst spills one $20 bill (`drops.ts` `TWENTY`, `centsOf`) that pays the wallet like the dimes. The hello carries each station's place and the survey's size for it, and a map of the water's edges (`waterside.ts` `WaterMap`, one bit per 100 m cell, base64), all kept in the world. The Caretaker is the valley's the same way (rule 13): stepped beside the shadowmen and sent in the same frame (`caretaker`, and `burned` where two beams burned it), in the maze the hello placed (`MazePlace`, kept in the world); its touch sends a `struck` frame with `by: 'caretaker'`, and it hunts only a raider a shadowman could rush. Where it is burned the valley spills two drops of gold bullion, a one troy ounce bar each (`drops.ts` `spillsOf`, the same `spill` action as the dimes, which names each drop's kind), into the taker's pack. Corpse runs are the valley's (rule 18, `corpses.ts`): a strike's `struck` frame is followed by a fall, alone in the valley (`ValleyDO` `fall`): the account's pack is emptied in one D1 batch (`PackStore.strip`, every kind left at zero but the key items, which stay in the pack), the wallet is read and the reducer's `spend` for the strike's share of it (`corpses.ts` `spilledDimes`) is taken out of it (`PackStore.purchase` with no item; if that fails, nothing spills) and left lying as a spilled drop of dimes where the raider fell, and the reducer lays a body (`Valley.corpses`, with what it holds and the account, never on the wire) where the raider's last state frame put them, one an account (a second fall takes the first body away, and what it still held is lost, whether or not it lays a new one), or the valley gives it all back (`PackStore.give`). `WorldWire.corpses` carries every body without its contents (`CorpseWire`), and the welcome and every `pack` frame carry the ids of the account's own (`corpses`). A `loot` frame names a body; only its account may, and the things go back into the pack before the body is gone (`fell` and `looted` snapshots). A key item never lies on a body: one left on a body laid before strikes kept them goes back into its account's pack as the account comes into the valley (the reducer's `keys-back`, `corpses.ts` `keysOff`, `ValleyDO` `keysBack`, giving the pack the keys before the body loses them), and a body left with nothing goes (a `looted` snapshot). The bodies are the valley's, not the world's, so neither the day's turn nor a world opened afresh takes them. The Recycling Center's research is the account's (rule 33, `recycling.ts`, D1 `research`, one row an account and line): the hello says where the wreck lies (kept in the world), a `research` frame answers with a `research` frame of the levels, and a `recycle` frame buys a line's next level only when the raider's last state frame put them within `CONFIG.recycling.tendReach` of the wreck (dialed in, from anywhere), the level and the empties out of the pack written in one batch; every socket on the account hears the `research` frame with its `line`, then the pack. Research never touches the shared world: loose change reads the taker's research as a `take-drop` lands, and only over dimes a burst left (`Drop.burst`, never a spilled wallet) adds as many again, on the valley's roll, to the taker's own wallet, with a `found` frame to the account; the drop and the `drop-taken` everyone sees are as they were. The Cabbage Stand is the account's (rule 23, `stand.ts`, D1 `stands`): the hello says where it stands (kept in the world), a `stand-stock`, `stand-collect` or `stand-upgrade` frame tends it only when the raider's last state frame put them within `CONFIG.stand.tendReach` of it, and the valley, alone (under its lock), reads the ledger, the pack and the wallet, judges the change, and writes all three in one transaction (`PackStore.tend`, guarded by the ledger's write count); a `stand` frame (the ledger, and the cents for a collect) goes to every socket on the account with a `pack` frame after, and the welcome carries the ledger. Erwin von Dutch is the valley's (rule 25, `dealer.ts`): the hello says where he sits (kept in the world), his stock today rides in `WorldWire.dealer` (full again when the day turns), and a `deal` frame names a good and what pays for it, only when the raider's last state frame put them within `CONFIG.dealer.dealReach` of him; the valley, alone (under its lock), reads the pack, judges the barter (`deal`: a good he has left, payment a shelf prices, enough of it at `CONFIG.dealer.rate`), and writes both sides in one transaction (`PackStore.barter`); everyone gets a `dealt` snapshot, the account a `pack` frame. No cash changes hands. Quests are the account's (rule 26, `quests.ts`, D1 `quests`): a `quest` frame names the quest and the step (`accept` beside Erwin, `lay` at the heart of the maze the world was opened with, `reward` beside Erwin), the valley, alone (under its lock), reads the stage and the pack, judges the step (`questStep`), and writes the stage and the pack change together (`PackStore.quest`, guarded by the stage it found); a `quest` frame (the stage) goes to every socket on the account with a `pack` frame after, and the welcome carries every quest's stage. The Undercroft is the valley's too (rule 11, `undercroft.ts`, `tunnelshades.ts`): the ground is a heightfield, so it is a sealed stone box raised `CONFIG.undercroft.lift` over a far corner of the survey square (`undercroftPlace`), its floor a registered floor and its stone walls on `walls`, so no one on the surface ever walks in; the trapdoor and the ladder move a raider there and back (client-only, `player.relocate`). The hello says where it lies (kept in the world); `stepShadows` keeps a raider down there out of the shadowmen's field and the Caretaker's reach, and steps the tunnel shades on its grid with the Caretaker's own pathing (`stepTunnelShades`), sending them in the same `shadowmen` frame (`tunnel`, each with `hurt`, the share of its hits taken; `tunnelBursts`, and `tunnelWounds` where the Warden took a hit it stands); a touch sends a `struck` frame with `by: 'tunnel'`, and each burst spills dimes like a shadowman's (but no tombstone) and counts as a burn for the daily task (the Warden earns its own XP, `warden`). The lookout tower by the maze's gate is out of every shadow's reach (rule 11, `lookout.ts`): it stands in the maze's frame, so the valley finds it from the `MazePlace` the hello already carries, and a raider whose last state frame puts them over its walled footprint is up on the deck, never `vulnerable` (`shadowRaiders`, `onLookout`), their beam still aimed; its deck is a registered floor and its ladder moves a raider up and down (client-only, `player.relocate`). Locks are the client's to keep for its own raider (`keys.ts`): the office's door is a gate on the walls (`walls.ts` `addGate`, `setGates`) that lets through whoever carries its key or stands behind it; the key itself is a key item in the pack, which the valley keeps, and key items are never dropped (`items.ts` `isKeyItem`; the valley refuses a `drop` of one). The stash is the account's (rule 19, `stash.ts`, D1 `stashes`): a `stow` or `unstow` frame moves units between the pack and the locker in one transaction (`PackStore.stow`; a key item is never stowed, `key-item`, and migration 0015 brought any stowed before back into the pack), only when the raider's last state frame put them within `CONFIG.stash.stationReach` of a station's pump island, and the welcome and every `pack` frame carry the locker (`stash`). Empties are the valley's too (rule 32, `recycling.ts` `emptyOf`): once the pack has given up a unit used or passed, the Worker reads what is left of that kind and runs an `empty` action, which puts the drink's can or bottle, or the finished cigarette pack, into the same pack. The laptop's files are the valley's (rule 31, `laptop.ts`, D1 `laptop_files`): a `laptop-list`, `laptop-read`, `laptop-save` or `laptop-delete` frame is heard only when the raider's last state frame put them within `CONFIG.laptop.stationReach` of a station's pump island (`atLaptop`; never dialed in, never from the bed), the valley reads and writes the files through `AccountStore` (`laptopFiles`, `laptopBody`, `saveLaptopFile`, `deleteLaptopFile`), a save alone for the account (`forAccount`) and judged against the writer's own files (`saveRefusal`, with the unique name index behind it), and answers that socket alone: `laptop-files` (every file without its text, each with its writer's username and whether it is the listener's, never an account), `laptop-file` (one file's text) and `laptop-saved` (the id it was kept under). Played alone only the readme is there. The season is the account's (rule 15, `season.ts`): `stepShadows` names the accounts credited with a burning (`creditedWith`, once per account), and the valley tallies each alone (under the account's lock), writing the progress and any reward in one D1 batch (`PackStore.score`, the `seasons` table); a `season` frame (`SeasonWire`, `rewarded` when it paid) goes to every socket on the account, a `pack` frame after a reward, and the welcome carries the progress. The daily task is the account's the same way (rule 16, `dailytask.ts`): each burst names the raiders whose beams held it (`shadowmen.ts` `BurstBy`, kept off the wire), `stepShadows` returns their accounts as `burned`, and the valley tallies each burn alone against the Central day (`tallyTask`), writing the progress and any reward in one D1 batch (`PackStore.scoreTask`, the `tasks` table, one row an account and task overwritten as the days turn); a `task` frame (`TaskWire`, `rewarded` when it paid) goes to every socket on the account, a `pack` frame after a reward, and the welcome carries the progress. The Book of Shadows is the account's too (rule 20, `book.ts`): the client sends a `discover` frame naming the entries it just came across (`actions.ts` `discover`, from `loop.ts` each frame and from the folk spoken to; anything found or already asked is left out), the valley writes the ones the account had not found, alone for the account (`forAccount`, `PackStore.discover`, the D1 `book` table, `newlyFound`), and sends a `book` frame naming them to every socket on the account; a frame too soon (10 in 10 seconds) or one it cannot write is nacked, and the client asks again a few seconds on. The welcome carries the whole book. Geometrie, the hit points, is the account's (rule 24, `health.ts`): the valley keeps what every wounded account has lost (`Valley.wounds`), so no reconnect or second tab heals, and sizes the whole by the account's level (`Member.level`, read from D1 on the join, moved on by a `level` action when the XP crosses into a new one, so the larger whole tops the raider up; `maxFor`). Each step's touches are tallied once an account, the heaviest striker if more than one landed (`stepShadows` `strikers`; `ValleyDO` `strike`, the reducer's `hit`, which takes `wards.ts` `damageOf` of the striker against the account's wards on a roll the valley passes in, `ValleyDO` `roll`): the touched sockets get a `struck` frame (what struck, the damage, what the wards took off, the geometrie left and whole) and the account's others a `health` frame, and the touch that takes the last of it (`Reduced.health.fatal`, `health: 0`) is followed by rule 18's fall, the account whole again after. Every `CONFIG.health.forecourt.everySeconds` the step gives back `CONFIG.health.forecourt.share` of whole to any raider whose last state frame put them on a forecourt (`forecourtMends`, the reducer's `mend` with no kind), so a forecourt heals over a couple of minutes, not at once, and a `use` of anything that heals (`items.ts` `healsPct`) gives its share of whole back once the pack has given the unit up; both, and a new level, send a `health` frame (left and whole) to every socket on the account, and every change to an account's geometrie sends everyone a `peer-health` frame (each of its socket ids, left and whole), so every raider sees every other's bar; each `PeerWire` carries the geometrie too (`health`, `max`, stamped from `Valley.wounds` as the valley sends it, `ValleyDO` `shown`). A hard landing is the client's word, since where a raider stands is its own: a `fall` frame names the height (`fall.ts` `isFallHeight`), sent just after the state frame that says where they came down, the reducer's `plunge` takes `fallDamage` of it, no ward cutting it, and the socket hears a `struck` frame `by: 'fall'`, a fatal one followed by rule 18's fall. The welcome carries the health. Citgos are the account's and the portals the valley's (rule 28, `citgos.ts`, `portals.ts`): each step of the shadowmen's clock writes the Citgo any placed raider on foot stands at, by their last state frame, when it is not yet the last their account stood at (`citgoVisits`, a `visit` action, `Valley.citgos`, kept like `places` whatever the world), and sends a `citgos` frame (the `CitgoLog`) to every socket on the account, paying a Citgo found past the first its bounty (`Reduced.earn`, written to the wallet before the log, so a failed write leaves the Citgo to be found again on the next step); the welcome carries the log. A `portal` frame opens a portal ahead of where the raider's last state frame put them (never the client's word), refused from the bed, with no state frame heard, with no Citgo yet, at the last Citgo itself, or with its ring full; its twin takes the first free slot of the ring on that Citgo's lot, where the hello placed every lot's ring (`rings`, kept in the world). Each is the account's one (`SharedWorld.portals`, the account never on the wire; another closes the first), opens with a `portal-opened` snapshot (`WorldMessage.portal` its id) and closes with a `portal-closed` one when its time is up (`settle`, and `wakeAt` wakes the valley for it); the day's turn leaves them, and a world opened afresh takes them. Walking through and fast travel are the client's alone, since where a raider stands is its own word (`actions.ts` `tendPortals`, `fastTravel`; `worldsync.ts` `portalNews`). The wards are the account's too (rule 27, `buffs.ts`): the valley keeps every account not sober (`Valley.wards`, on its clock in seconds), dosed by each `use` once the pack has given the unit up (the reducer's `dose`), and sobered by a shattering; a `wards` frame (the levels at that moment) goes to every socket on the account, and the welcome carries them. It aims each raider's beam reached out by their high (`shadowRaiders`, `buffedBeam`), and each level wards its own shadows' hits (rule 24, `wards.ts`). A `pass` frame names a kind and a peer id: the reducer's `pass` checks both raiders on foot and in reach by their last state frames, the giver's pack gives the unit up, then `dose` with `to` doses the taker in full and the giver their share and grants the giver `share` XP, the taker gets the item's heal, and each side gets a `passed` frame naming the other. The stimulated's sprint is the client's alone. The raider's level is the account's too (rule 22, `progression.ts`): one XP bar fed by everything, each grant named by the reducer (`Reduced.xp`, and `stepShadows`' `xp` for a burn, a spider and a burning; `XpGrant`, once per account) and worth what `progression.ts` `XP` says: a burst in the raider's beam, the Caretaker burned (the big jump), a ride with Marx that gets home with them in the bed or lets them off where they picked, over a drive of `CONFIG.truck.rideXpMetres` or more (`marx.ts` `rideMetres`), the day's berry, a pickup, a unit bought, a drop the valley left taken up (`Drop.spilled`; never a raider's own drop), and a collect from the Cabbage Stand, once for every whole `CONFIG.stand.xpCents` it pays out (`stand.ts` `standXp`, `XpGrant.times`), so small collects never earn more than one big one. The valley adds each account's sum in one statement (`PackStore.gainXp`, the `levels` table, no lock needed), sends an `xp` frame (the XP in all and what it gained) to every socket on the account, and, when that crosses into a new level (`CONFIG.progression`, `levelUp`), a `peer-updated` frame to everyone; the welcome carries the XP, and each `PeerWire` the level (read from D1 on the hello, never from the client). Every change comes to every client as a whole `WorldWire` snapshot with a reason; `worldsync.ts` reads it for this raider (`settledBy` whether a take, a sale or a drop was theirs, `aboard`, `seatOf`, `countdown`, `newLeg`), and `valleysync.ts` applies it. The Citgo shelves are shared too (rule 7): the valley holds every station's stock unit by unit (`WorldWire.shelves`, indexed like `world.fuelPoints`; each item a row of on-the-shelf flags, one per slot), a `buy` frame asks for the unit the buyer is looking at and the `bought` snapshot settles the race; cash is the account's wallet in D1 (`wallets`, a new account starts with $40); a buy runs alone in the valley (under its lock), which reads the wallet, refuses a sale it does not cover, and charges it and fills the pack in one transaction (`PackStore.purchase`), so a failed write never takes the cash without the item. The client settles the sale locally (`shop.ts` `settle`) once the valley confirms it, and the valley's `pack` frame (the pack and the cash) has the last word. A client whose station count differs is turned away like one whose pickups differ. The berry bushes are the valley's too (rule 8): the one at the spawn Citgo (bush 0) and the ring at the maze's heart (1 to 6, `CONFIG.maze.bushes`), one berry a day each per account; a `collect` frame names the bush, the `daily` reply says whether a berry came off it, and the welcome carries which bushes the account has had today's berry off. The valley remembers only the accounts that have had today's berry off each bush (keyed by `sharedworld.ts` `bushKey`), so two sockets on one account share one berry a bush. Every item a raider carries is their account's pack, in D1 (`packs`, `worker/d1packs.ts`), and the valley alone writes it (rule 10): the reducer says what goes in or out (`Reduced.pack`: a berry, a pickup, a unit bought, a drop taken up) and a `use` frame takes one out; the welcome carries the pack (everything of the account's it carries is read in one D1 batch, `PackStore.welcome`), and every change sends a `pack` frame to each socket on the account. The client applies its own change at once and takes the valley's pack as the truth when it lands. Drops are the valley's too (rule 12): a `drop` frame names the kind and how many, the valley sets it down ahead of where the raider's last state frame put them (never where the frame says, `drops.ts` `dropSpot`), and it stands only once the account's pack has given the units up (`setDown`, alone in the valley like a buy). `WorldWire.drops` carries every drop with its id, and a `take-drop` frame asks for one, first to ask wins. The oldest of what the valley itself spilled goes past `CONFIG.drops.spilledMax`; a raider's own drops lie until the day turns. The hello carries every pickup's kind, count and place (`PickupSpec`), so the valley knows what a take is worth and who is in reach of it, and where each berry bush stands. Moab trades cosmetics for what the pack holds (rule 14, `cosmetics.ts`): a `trade` frame names the offer, the valley reads the pack, refuses a trade it does not cover or a cosmetic already had, and takes the price and writes the cosmetic in one transaction (`PackStore.trade`, alone in the valley like a buy; D1 `cosmetics`). The account's cosmetics ride in the welcome and every `pack` frame, and on each `PeerWire` (read from D1 on the hello, never from the client); a trade sends everyone a `peer-updated` frame so the halo shows. A cosmetic can open a character instead (`coleman-1` opens `coleman1`): the hello, the `appearance` frame and `PUT /auth/look` each refuse a character the account has not traded for (`characters.ts` `mayRaidAs`, against the cosmetics the valley or D1 holds, never the client's word). Account ids never go on the wire. Shared moments are server timestamps read through `clock.ts`, so every client agrees where the truck is. A client whose `world.pickups` differ from the world's, in number or in any pickup's kind or count, is turned away (4005). Positions are not compared, so bump `PROTOCOL_VERSION` whenever placement changes: a new protocol opens the world afresh.

Bull Valley Dial-Up is the valley on a phone (rule 29): a raider may say a `dial` instead of a `hello` (`protocol.ts` `DialMessage`: the protocol and the outfit, nothing of the world), and the valley joins them with no body (`Member.dialup`, the reducer's `dial`): no world is opened for them (the welcome's `world` is null in a valley no 3D raider has opened one in), the truck pays them no mind, and their state and headlights frames are dropped, so they are never placed and no shadow reaches them. Space is collapsed for them: their locker and their Cabbage Stand answer wherever they are, world or none, Erwin deals with them and gives and rewards the rose from anywhere, and they buy off the shelves of any Citgo their account has stood at (`not-visited` otherwise). A raider dialed in uses what the pack holds as anyone does (a `use` frame: the valley doses the wards and mends the geometrie), to ready a run. Whatever else takes a body (the truck, a take, a berry, a pass, a drop or taking one up, a body, a portal, laying the rose) is refused them with `dialup` (`sharedworld.ts` `bodiless`). `PeerWire.dialup` tells everyone: the 3D client never draws them (they send no state), the log says they dialed in, and `/online` and `/friends` say they are on the phone (`friends.ts` `whereabouts`). The phone's side is its own app (`entry.ts` picks it for a device that only touches, or `?dialup`): it opens on a modem that dials the valley with a synthesized handshake and prints CONNECT only once the welcome comes (`modem.ts`), then a shell built for a thumb (`dialshell.ts`, `dialpack.ts`, `dialbook.ts`, `src/dialup.css`, in the house style: eggshell on black, panels on a hard shadow, the Citgo red for what can be pressed): the lobby (the raider in their character on a turntable a drag turns, with their level, XP, the season, today's task, the book and the Citgos found, `dialstate.ts` `raiderStats`; the Cabbage Stand; the locker), the pack (stow, the hotbar), the Citgo (the shelves of every Citgo stood at, `dialcitgo.ts`), Erwin's barter and the rose's first and last steps (the 3D valley's own dialog, `dealdialog.ts`), Gron (`grondialog.ts`), Moab and his trade (`dialmoab.ts`), Social (the chat, whispers and friends, `dialsocial.ts`), the Book of Shadows (each page's portrait the 3D valley's own model turning, dark until found), and Expeditions (sharedworld rule 30, `dialexpeditions.ts`), all read off the valley's frames (`dialstate.ts`).

Idle expeditions are Dial-Up's own way to earn (rule 30, `expeditions.ts`, D1 `expeditions`): an `expedition-send` frame names a run (Ride with Marx, Sweep the Corn, Down the Undercroft, `CONFIG.expeditions`), and the valley, alone (under its lock), reads the account's run, refuses a second (`busy`), one the level has not opened (`locked`), one the wallet does not cover (`short`: each run's buy-in, `CONFIG.expeditions.runs[...].cost`, the Ride free) or one from a raider on foot (`not-dialup`), draws the run's seed (`ValleyDO` `roll`) and writes it with the matching ward's level as it left and its buy-in out of the wallet in the same batch (`PackStore.embark`, guarded by the row's write count and the wallet's CHECK). An `expedition-claim` once its time is up (`out` before) settles what the seed brings home (`outcomeOf`), writes the haul into the pack and the wallet with the run left empty in one transaction (`PackStore.homecoming`), earns its XP (`XP.expedition`, so many times) and takes a hurt run's geometrie off the account, never the last point (rule 24). An `expedition` frame (the run, and the outcome for a claim) goes to every socket on the account, a `pack` frame after a claim, and the welcome carries the run. The 3D models (`dial3d.ts`: `avatarview.ts`, `itemthumbs.ts`, `bookportraits.ts`) load on their own after the modem connects, so the modem never waits on three.js.
