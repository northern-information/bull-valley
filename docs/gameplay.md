# Gameplay

Spawn at the gas station with your wallet (a new account starts with $40), or wherever your account last stood on foot. Every 30 to 90 seconds a few slurms (black slugs, after the worms of _Out of This World_, each with one white tooth it bares and takes back in) drop off a Citgo's dripline, the store's front eave or an edge of the pump canopy, sometimes several at once; David Carlsten comes out from behind the counter with his bat, smashes each one, curses in the log of whoever is near (`COPY.toml` `[slurms]`, in turn), and walks back in. Scenery only: they never touch a raider, and everyone at that Citgo sees the same drop (`slurms.ts`, on the shared clock). A slurm still crawling squashes under any raider's foot (`slurms.ts` `treads`, `underfoot`; every client judges it from the feet it sees, ours and each peer's last frame, so nothing goes on the wire); one squashed before Carlsten reaches it is off his list, and he goes after the nearest left, or, with none left for him, stops where he is, says his thanks in the log of whoever is near (`COPY.toml` `slurms.thanks`) instead of his curse, turns round and walks back in the way he came (`slurms.ts` `afterTrodden`; all of them gone before he leaves the counter, he never comes out), and Castle Entropy's moat has its own, dropping off the rim, the island's edge and the cranes' hooks to crawl the floor, lie a while and melt into the concrete (`moatslurms.ts`, `CONFIG.slurms.moat`). The valley's music, the Bull Valley Scaduscope theme, loops quietly under everything from the main menu on, and from Begin on the valley's sounds (so far the strikes, the flashlight, the burns and bursts, the spiders and the Caretaker, heard from where they happen) play over it; a Music and a Sounds slider in the main menu's Audio and on the pause overlay set how loud. Shop the Citgo: cigarettes and joints on the counter, medicine on the rack, and the drinks behind the glass doors of the coolers along the back but the liquor (Wild Turkey, Jim Beam, Grey Goose), which stands at room temperature on an open shelf beside them, 3 of everything a day and 20 Ice Mountain. Each unit floats its name and price as you look at it (dim when the wallet falls short), and `E` on it buys it outright, no window, so you can jam `E` down a cooler. `E` at David Carlsten behind the counter opens the Citgo's trade window, the same window as Erwin von Dutch's and Moab Coldë's: your side is the wallet (the Citgo takes cash, to the cent, so there is nothing to put down or even up), his side every kind on the shelves with its price and how many are left today, and Trade buys one. Matthew Marx's white Chevy is parked by the spawn Citgo, Marx reading at the tailgate; he reads for five minutes, then crosses Lake Avenue to the field between the road and the corn maze and does donuts for five, then drives home and reads again, over and over, and anyone arriving in the valley while he is at his donuts brings him straight home. Whenever the truck rolls, Day of Justice plays out of the cab (`truckradio.ts`, `CONFIG.truck.radio`, at the Music setting): as cut in the bed, and out of it fading and losing its highs with the distance, so from the Citgo only the lows of his donuts carry. At his donuts his rear wheels throw clods of grass and dirt and cut ruts in the field that heal slowly while he reads (`donutwake.ts`, `CONFIG.truck.donuts.spray` and `ruts`). Climb into the bed (`E`) and a 60-second countdown starts for everyone, and the first one in gets the map (`citgomap.ts`, opened for a charter): a click anywhere picks where he goes (snapped to the nearest road), `M` leaves it to him, and `E` hops out. When the countdown runs out he takes whoever is in the bed there and lets them off, then drives home empty, or with no pick on the joyride out through the valley and home again, letting them off at the Citgo. With no one else in the valley no countdown shows: he leaves as soon as the pick is made. Hop out (`E`) anywhere. With his whistle in hand (he gives it the first time you talk to him), the click whistles for him from anywhere while his bed is empty: he drives to you, you climb in, and he drives you home. Find cabbages in the wilderness and in the shade under the dishes behind the spawn Citgo (`cabbages.ts` `placeDishCabbages`, `CONFIG.cabbage.underDishes`), and they go into your pack, under Loot. Pickups, the Citgo shelves and whatever lies dropped all come back with the day, at midnight Central. Tab opens the pack over the valley with the pointer free, the raider standing on a turntable beside it in their character and whatever they wear (`avatarview.ts`; the Flaming Halo too), on its Consumables tab (Loot, where berries and cabbages go, Key Items, and Materials, where the empties go, sit beside it; `A`/`D` switch): a drink drunk leaves its empty can or bottle in the pack, and the last cigarette out of a pack leaves the empty pack (`recycling.ts`, passed or used, in the pack it came out of, the 3D valley and Dial-Up alike), for the Recycling Center at the wreck; hovering an item shows its card (a small page of the Book of Shadows: the item spinning, its words, and its stats off the table, `itemstats.ts`: its kind, what it heals, how far it moves each ward and what that ward wards against, how long it works, how many to one, its shelf price), `E` uses it, `1`–`9` puts it on the hotbar along the bottom of the screen, whose number keys use it from then on, and `X` drops one (the open pack or bottle, or one of anything else) a little ahead of you, Shift-`X` the whole stack. `1`–`9` with no item under the cursor empties that slot, and a kind the pack runs out of (the last one used, dropped or stowed) leaves the bar on its own. The mouse works the open pack too (`packhud.ts` `wireMouse`, `bindings.ts` `PACK_MOUSE`): a click pins an item's card so the keys act on it, a double-click uses it, a drag puts it on a hotbar slot (every slot shows while the pack is open; a slot dragged off the bar or right-clicked empties), a right-click opens a menu of everything it can do, and at the locker a Shift-click moves the whole stack. `E` answers only what the raider can see: anything it acts on (a pickup, a shelf unit, someone to talk to, a body, a bush, the lockers, the laptop, the stand, the truck, a ladder, a hatch, a door, the heart) must be within reach, some of it about their own level, inside `CONFIG.player.pickupCone` of where they look (wider for a wide thing), with no wall between (`interactions.ts` `sightAngle`, `SIGHTS`; `walls.ts` `between`, where a low fixture like a counter stands between nothing), and of the pickups the one nearest the middle of the view first; so nothing sells through the back room's partition. Anyone can take a drop up with `E`, as with any pickup; drops lie there until the day turns. The raider's state of mind is four wards, shown in the upper left as a red tetrahedron turning on its point (`vitalshud.ts`, `wards.ts` `tetraView`), each corner glowing with its level and carrying its mark (`icons.ts`: a weed leaf for high, a pill for stimulated, a beer bottle for drunk, a blotter tab for tripping): high the joint (and a little off the psychedelics), stimulated cigarettes, the energy drinks and anything swallowed as a pill (Adderall, aspirin, ibuprofen, Benadryl), drunk beer and liquor (Four Loko does both, and water sobers a little), tripping LSD and mushrooms (eye drops bring high and tripping down); each fades on its own (`CONFIG.wards.fadePerSecond`). Each is a layer of armor against its own shadows (`wards.ts`, `CONFIG.wards`): high against the shadowmen, stimulated against the spiders and spiderlings, drunk against the Caretaker, tripping against the tunnel shades and the Warden, the matching corner cutting a touch by up to 60% and the strongest other by up to 10% more; a chip under the bar for every ward going shows its strength. High also reaches the flashlight's beam further and wider, and stimulated quickens the sprint (`buffs.ts`, `CONFIG.buffs`). Every buff going (each of the four wards, and the joint's perception) shows as a cell in a row over the hotbar (`buffbar.ts` `activeBuffs`, `buffhud.ts`), its left edge on the bar's first slot (or, with nothing on the bar, where the bar would be), its mark under a dark wedge that grows as it winds down and the time left under it. Anything smoked, drunk or swallowed can be passed instead (`G`: over an item in the open pack, or in the valley the last thing used) to a raider on foot within `CONFIG.buffs.passReach` in front of you: they take it in full, its dose and its heal, and the giver catches a share of the dose (`shareScale`) and XP for sharing. Anything smoked or drunk also blurs the view for a second as it goes down, and three of the wards work on the view for as long as they last, each at full strength from half its level up and thinning as it fades (`wardview.ts`, drawn by `trails.ts`, tuned in `CONFIG.wardView`): drunk trails it, each frame keeping most of the last so that anything that moves, the view included, smears behind itself; high pushes every colour further from grey, more vivid, with a little of the drunk's trails; stimulated sharpens every edge. Shadowmen cross the valley around every raider, the same ones for everyone; one that passes close rushes, and in reach it draws back, winds up (`CONFIG.shadowmen.windupSeconds`) and lunges, every shadow the same and for everyone to see; a lunge lands only if you are still in reach, so a step back makes it miss. Every raider has geometrie, their hit points: 1,000 at level 1 and 40 more a level (`health.ts` `maxGeometrie`, `CONFIG.health`; 4,920 at level 99, and on up, since the raider's level has no top), shown Elden Ring style as a thin bar beside the tetrahedron in the upper left, deep crimson in a dull gilt hairline with no numbers on it, as long as the raider's whole (it grows with the level), a yellow trail of what a touch took draining after the fill, and climbing back over a beat when geometrie is given back. Every other raider's geometrie shows too: a thin bar under their name tag (`peers.ts`) and, under your own, a list of the nearest few, each name over its bar (`partyhud.ts`). Each shadow's touch takes its own hit (`CONFIG.damage`: a spiderling 140, a shadowman 320, a tunnel shade 420, a spider 480, the Caretaker 750, the Warden 1,100, each give or take 10% on the valley's roll), cut by the wards (`wards.ts` `damageOf`); the number pops under the bar with what the wards took off it in the ward's colour (`hud.ts` `popDamage`, touches close together adding up), the view washes red and shakes, the first ward to take the edge off says so in the log, and the touch leaves you where you stand with a few seconds' grace (`CONFIG.health`). The touch that takes the last of it shatters your geometrie (GEOMETRIE SHATTERED over the static) and puts you back at the spawn Citgo, whole, with an empty pack but for its key items, which stay with you as Elden Ring's do: everything else you carried stays on your body where you fell (a corpse run). Standing on any Citgo forecourt gives geometrie back a little every couple of seconds until you are whole (`CONFIG.health.forecourt`: from nothing in a little under two minutes), so a heal from the pack is the quick way, and everything smoked, drunk or swallowed gives geometrie back as it moves the wards (`items.ts` `healsPct`, a share of whole: 15% for a cigarette, a drink, water or a pill, 30% for the joint, LSD, mushrooms, Four Loko and the liquor; never past whole). The locker comes through untouched, but the wallet comes open as the raider goes down: a share of it (`CONFIG.corpses.walletShare`, `corpses.ts` `spilledDimes`) lies in dimes where they fell, a drop like a burst's, for whoever gets there first, and the log says how much. Your body lies there, in the outfit you wore, your name floating over it as over a raider standing, for everyone to see (one at a time: die again before you reach it and it is gone, with everything on it), until you run back and take your things back with `E` over it; no one else can, and it lies through the day's turn. Behind the shelves of every Citgo a doorway at the back leads into a back room with a bank of lockers under an American flag, health and safety notices on the walls, and a bathroom walled off at its far end (a toilet, a sink under its mirror, EMPLOYEES MUST WASH HANDS, and a laptop on a card table against the wall, its screen the only light in the room, washing it blue, though a flashlight brought in lights it too, `bathglow.ts`); to the right of the lockers a steel back door stands open under an EXIT sign onto a concrete pad behind the store, a dumpster on it (`plazamodel.ts` `buildDumpster`, instanced with the store): `E` in front of them opens the pack with a Locker tab, your stash (the same at every Citgo, and no strike reaches it; a key item never goes in); `F` over an item stows one (the open pack or bottle, or one of anything else), Shift-`F` the stack, and on the Locker tab `F` and Shift-`F` take them back out. `E` at the laptop sits down at it (`laptopdialog.ts`): its screen fills the view, open on a text editor, white on dark blue, with the readme up (`LAPTOP.TXT`, always the first file, formatting kept as typed). Esc closes the editor onto an XP desktop, green hills under a blue sky with a taskbar and a start menu, and a window that browses `C:\Documents and Settings`, a folder for every raider: anyone's file opens to read, the raider's own to write and save (Ctrl+S), New Text Document makes another (up to `CONFIG.laptop.perAccount`), and Delete takes one of theirs away. Getting up (`E` on the bare desktop, or Turn Off Computer; Esc steps back inside the computer and never gets up) leaves it for the next one on the editor and the readme again. The files are the valley's, the same at every Citgo. Every raider carries a flashlight: with it in hand the left button raises it and turns it on, and again puts it down; a shadowman held in anyone's beam for half a second bursts into black mist, for everyone, and leaves 1 to 8 dimes lying where it stood (`CONFIG.shadowmen.dimes`); anyone can take them up with `E`, like a drop, and they go into the wallet at 10¢ each, never the pack, with a line in the log. Every enemy wears an Elden Ring health bar over it once it is hurt, to anyone within thirty metres (`enemybars.ts`, `CONFIG.healthBars`): a shadowman, spider or spiderling, the Caretaker and the tunnel shades drain under a beam and fill back as it comes off, the Warden's keeps the hits it has taken, and a topiary's the blows of a torch. Marx's headlights burn them the same way, parked or driving: one held in them bursts, dimes and all, though no one is credited with it (`CONFIG.truck.headlights`). Among them come the shadow spiders: black spiders twice a shadowman's height on long legs arched high over their bodies, eight red eyes burning through the fog, coming up now and then anywhere (about one arrival in twenty, as often as when the lure was water) and far more often round their lairs, the valley's works gone to rust and concrete: the dish array behind the spawn Citgo, Castle Entropy and Bull Valley Plaza (`lairs.ts`, `CONFIG.shadowmen.spider`); one takes twice as long in a beam to burst, rushes a little faster, touches from further off, and leaves a $20 bill, cash the same way, and breaks into a brood of 3 to 8 spiderlings (`CONFIG.shadowmen.spiderling`): small, quick spiders that rush whoever is near from further off, touch for a block like any shadow, burst in a blink in a beam, and leave a few dimes each, never a tombstone, never counted toward the daily task or XP, never breaking again. A shadowman (and only a shadowman: no spider, spiderling, tunnel shade or the Warden) leaves a tombstone a step from its dimes, carved with the name it is given then (the Bull Valley Scaduscope's folklore names, carried over from forgotten-industries into `names.ts`), seen by everyone; the tombstones stay when the day turns, the oldest going past `CONFIG.graves.max`. An item used from the pack or the hotbar comes up in the right hand the same way, and goes down again. The valley keeps every Citgo each account has stood on the forecourt of (within `CONFIG.portal.visitReach` of its pump island), and the last; each one found past the first (the spawn Citgo) pays a bounty into the wallet once (`CONFIG.bounties.citgo`, $5) and XP, and finding Mt. Coleman's Keep (its page in the Book of Shadows) pays `CONFIG.bounties.keep` ($25) and XP once (`book.ts` `bountyOf`). With the Citgo phone in hand the click brings it up, and it dials a Citgo portal (`CONFIG.portal.dialSeconds`; the click again hangs up, and a touch knocks it down): a ring of cold blue light opens a few steps ahead, facing you, and its twin opens in a ring of slots on the lot of the last Citgo you stood at, each end with the name of the raider who dialed it floating over it, for everyone to see. Anyone on foot who walks into either end comes out in front of the other, facing away from it, so a raider can go back to their Citgo and return, and anyone at that Citgo can step out to whoever opened it. Each account keeps one open at a time (dialing another closes the first), for three minutes (`CONFIG.portal.lifeSeconds`); none opens from the bed, before any Citgo is visited, or at the Citgo it would go to, and the phone says how long yours has left. `M` opens the map over the valley with the pointer free (`M` closes it): the survey's roads from above, every Citgo (each named for its nearest road), the ones visited lit in Citgo red, the last ringed and named, and you an arrow among them, with a bone-white cross named Your body wherever your own body lies until you take your things back off it. The wheel zooms the map in about the pointer and a drag pans it; a drag never pins or travels. Standing on any Citgo forecourt or at a payphone found, a click on a visited Citgo fast travels there, setting you down in the middle of its portal ring facing the pumps, and a click on a payphone found sets you down in front of it. At the heart of the corn maze six more berry bushes ring the portal, each with a berry a day for every account like the one at the spawn Citgo, and the Caretaker keeps the maze: a hooded shade with a cold lantern that floats down the paths, out to a random corner and home to the heart by turns. One that sees you in the maze (or feels you very close) hunts you down the paths, faster than a walk and slower than a sprint, and in reach it draws its lantern back flaring, then lurches out, and its touch costs a block like a shadowman's. One flashlight does nothing to it; two raiders' beams on it at once for a second or so burn it, leaving two bars of gold bullion (one troy ounce each) lying side by side where it was (`CONFIG.caretaker.bullion`), each a drop anyone can take up with `E` into the pack, under Loot, and it forms again at the heart three minutes later. The Overlook's hedge animals stand in the maze too (`topiaries.ts`, `topiaryrig.ts`, `CONFIG.topiaries`), after the novel: two lions, a dog, a rabbit and a buffalo of clipped privet, each broadside across a corridor along the road so nothing gets by it, and a corridor with one in it is shut until it goes. They are not shadows: no beam burns them, they never strike, and they are there to frighten and to stand in the way. Every seventy seconds or so on the valley's clock each goes to another of its own spots, all over the maze and none near the heart or the gate, staggered so they never all go at once, and every client reads the same clock and puts them in the same places with nothing on the wire. But no raider ever sees one move: it goes only while you look at neither where it stood nor where it is going (in view, within sight and with no corn between; up the lookout the corn hides nothing), and never onto you, so it waits while you stare at it and is gone, or closer, or standing where the way was open, when you turn back, with a rustle of hedge if it lands within earshot (`topiary-rustle`). Akashic shows one of each. Only fire harms them. A red toolbox stands at the foot of the lookout's ladder, spare torches standing up out of it (`torchmodel.ts`, `CONFIG.torch`): `E` at it, looking at it, puts a lit torch in your pack, one an account, a key item kept through a death, and it goes on the hotbar. Selected, it comes up into view in your hand from the bottom right, drawn the way the phone is (`torchhand.ts`: the same hand in the same flat pixel polygons, the fist round a pine stake, the rag's fire licking on top), and a click swings it, drawing back and chopping down across the view; a swing that lands on a topiary within reach takes a bite out of it (twenty or so of its hundred, give or take, on the valley's roll), shudders it and hisses, and an Elden Ring bar shows over its back to anyone within thirty metres once it is hurt (`healthbar.ts`, the bar the raiders wear). Every raider's swings come off the same health, which the valley keeps. Five or so blows burn it away: it bursts into flame like a haystack, topples over onto its back, kicks its legs in the air while it blackens, and burns down into the ground (`topiaries.ts` `deathPose`, `CONFIG.topiaries.deathSeconds`), its corridor opens, and three minutes later it grows back whole where the clock has it then, the moment nobody near is looking. A torch harms nothing else, and no beam harms a topiary. Just out from the maze's gate, past the ENTER! sign, a timber lookout tower stands on four splayed legs (`lookout.ts`, `CONFIG.maze.lookout`): `E` at the ladder's foot climbs to its deck, 16 m up, where the corn lies open below (as far as the fog lets you see), and `E` at the top climbs back down. Nothing reaches a raider up there, no shadowman and not the Caretaker, though their beam still burns what comes below. Straight out the spawn Citgo's front door, across Lake Avenue past the donut field, Castle Entropy floats over its moat (`moat.ts`, `castle.ts`, `castlesite.ts`, `CONFIG.castle`): an irregular pentagon of poured concrete cut ten stories into the terrain itself and about as wide, a trickle of water down its floor, pipes, valves and vines down its walls, crumbling cranes leaning out over it from the rim, and zig-zag fire escapes on the outer walls that a raider walks down flight by flight to the floor (`Ground.standAt` keeps the feet on the flight they are on, and a rail between the two lanes keeps them from stepping across onto the flight over or under theirs). Concrete is poured 27 m out round the rim, out to its angular corners and stopping short of Industrial Heights Drive where the road runs by, its formwork still staked along the far edge, and an unfinished fence stands on it all the way round (`castleyard.ts`, `castleyardmodel.ts`, `CONFIG.castle.yard`): two staggered rows of grey poles some twenty feet long, each straight to its knee and bent about thirty degrees in toward the castle, gaps here and there, the tops of the fire escapes and the cranes' footings left clear. On a good share of them a span has broken loose and hangs in the air over the break, bobbing, shedding flakes, and every so often crumbling away to nothing, gone a while, then coming back (on the shared clock, so everyone sees the same span go). Behind the fence lie the supplies (stacked I-beams on timber, crates, heaps of broken concrete with rebar through them, cable spools, culvert pipes, pallets of cement bags), each side has a light tower throwing a cold pool on the concrete, which ends at the lip and never spills down the wall (one in a while sputters), and on one side stands a lit payphone. Stand at it and it is found for the account (the log says so): it goes on the map as a pale blue dot, and fast travel leaves from it and comes to it as it does a visited Citgo, setting a raider down in front of it. The pipes along the walls bend out round each fire escape's outer rail rather than through it, with no bracket or junction box reaching back into its lanes (`moat.ts` `pipeFittings`), and they are a sparse maze, not a route: down every wall, level under level from just under the rim to near the floor, 2.5 to 3.2 m apart (more than a body climbs, so the maze only goes down, and a drop from one level to the next barely hurts), each level broken into runs 4 to 14 m long with gaps between, each run in one of three rows out from the wall; the first under the rim is laid nine runs in ten, to catch a raider who walks off, and those under it only one in four (`CONFIG.castle.pipes`). A run is laid only where the rim over it stands at least 2.5 m higher, so wherever the rim runs, the first pipe under it is near. A raider who walks off the rim falls past a pipe close enough to catch it (`moat.ts` `catchPipe`, within `CONFIG.fall.catchReach` of its top) and lands on it hurt instead of dead, clinging a moment (`CONFIG.fall.steadySeconds`) before they can move; nine in ten who walk off are caught short of a fatal fall. One who sprints off sails out past most of them. From there the way down is the maze: walk a run to its end and drop to one under it in another row, round the fire escapes too; a missing run is a long fall. A body too far under a pipe to step up onto it is stopped by it like any wall, and a run on the lowest level may turn down to the floor, a post there. The moat's slurms fall past the pipes to the floor. No two fire escapes are alike: each turn lands a little on or short of the last, so the flights run longer or shorter at one pitch, and each landing runs on past its turn by more or less (`CONFIG.castle.stairs.vary`), railed across its far end but at the foot. The shadowmen walk the ground at its height and reach no higher or lower than a body: one on the floor cannot touch a raider up a fire escape or on the rim, nor one on the rim a raider below, and a flashlight shone down off the rim burns what walks the floor (`shadowmen.ts` `reachesUp`). None walks through the moat's walls: a shadowman at the rim or the island's edge turns along it, and goes after a raider within 60 m at the other height by the nearest fire escape, treading its flights and landings down to the floor or up to the rim, and giving it up if he has not reached it in half a minute; a spider or a spiderling crawls straight down or up the wall's face instead, crossing or hunting, clinging to it head first the way it goes (`shadowclimb.ts`, `CONFIG.shadowmen.wall`, `CONFIG.shadowmen.climb`). The island in the middle cannot be reached yet. The castle hangs over it on its rock, an old gothic church in dark stone (steep slate gables, buttress piers, lancets and rose windows of smoke-dark glass, pinnacles and gargoyles, the cathedral's spire over all) grown through with concrete swollen like a pillow and crowned with the forms of the Yugoslav spomeniks as Villeneuve might have poured them (a sheer grooved monolith leaning off true, a star of fins, a cantilevered wing, a faceted boulder split in two), all in greys, blacks and a little brown but for the lighthouse's blue, made anew every night at midnight Central (`castleOf`, seeded by the day, nothing on the wire), and coming apart all the while (`crumble.ts`, on the shared clock, so everyone sees the same stone fall): bits always falling off it and fading as they go, and every so often a room, a wing or a flying buttress crumbling away in a spray of chunks, gone a while, then growing back. Its lighthouse turns three blue beams that show across the valley through the fog. The rim is open: where the ground drops away the feet fall (`fall.ts`, `CONFIG.fall`), and a landing from more than a story takes a share of geometrie that grows to all of it at two stories, which shatters it like any strike. The valley runs as a live service, one season at a time: Season One is **The Caretaker**. Every raider whose beam was on the Caretaker as it came apart is credited with the burning, and the fifth pays $100 into the wallet and a carton of Marlboro Reds (ten packs) into the pack, once. The season going live is said once in the log, in red, at the first Begin; a banner sweeps across the view at each burning and when the season is done; a tracker in the upper right, out of sight but for a few seconds each time a count on it goes up, and a card on the intro and pause overlay keep the count. Beside it runs a daily task, the same for every account each Central day: burn 5 shadows (shadowmen, spiders, tunnel shades and the Warden all count, never a spiderling; every raider whose beam was on one as it burst is credited), and the fifth pays $5 into the wallet, once a day; a row under the season in the tracker keeps today's count, and the count starts over at midnight Central. Under it a third row keeps the raider's level: one XP bar for the account that everything feeds (burning a shadowman or a spider, burning the Caretaker, a ride with Marx of some length, berries, pickups, purchases, taking up what the valley leaves lying, passing something to another raider, and collecting from the Cabbage Stand, by the cents it pays); each new level sweeps a banner across the view and adds a line to the log, and the level shows on everyone's name tag and in `/online`. `E` on another raider in reach and in front of you (the same reach and cone `G` passes to), or `/inspect name` for anyone, in the valley or not, opens their card (`inspect.ts`, `inspectdialog.ts`): their figure turning in their character, guitar finish and cosmetics, their level, how much of the Book of Shadows they have found, how far through the season they are, whether they are in the valley, and a button to ask them to be friends (or accept their asking); on the phone, a name tapped on the Social tab's online list opens the same card. Raiders play together in a party of up to four: `/invite name` asks a raider in the valley (or the card's Invite to Party), inviting back joins them, `/leave` leaves, `/party` lists it, and `/p message` says a line to it alone, inked cyan. A party's members see each other on the map (`M`) as named cyan arrows, the party list under the geometrie bar shows them rather than whoever is nearest, and when the Caretaker burns, every member standing near a burner is credited with the burning too. A dime a shadowman spills is still whoever's gets there first. A dropped line keeps its place in the party through the linger; on the phone, the Social tab shows the party, its invites with Join, and Leave. A crew is for good: `/crew create TAG Name` starts one for $100.00 out of the wallet (a tag is 2 to 4 letters or digits, and a tag or a name another crew has is refused), its leader invites with `/crew invite name` or the card's Invite to Crew (in the valley or not; the invite waits a week), `/crew join TAG` takes an invite up, `/crew leave` leaves (a leader who leaves hands the crew to whoever has been in it longest, and the last one out ends it), `/crew kick name` puts a member out, `/crew` lists it, and `/c message` speaks to it alone, inked amber.
