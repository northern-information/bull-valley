# The MVP loop

Spawn at the gas station with your wallet (a new account starts with $40), or wherever your account last stood on foot. Every 30 to 90 seconds a few slurms (black slugs, after the worms of _Out of This World_, each with one white tooth it bares and takes back in) drop off a Citgo's dripline, the store's front eave or an edge of the pump canopy, sometimes several at once; David Carlsten comes out from behind the counter with his bat, smashes each one, curses in the log of whoever is near (`COPY.toml` `[slurms]`, in turn), and walks back in. Scenery only: they never touch a raider, and everyone at that Citgo sees the same drop (`slurms.ts`, on the shared clock). A slurm still crawling squashes under any raider's foot (`slurms.ts` `treads`, `underfoot`; every client judges it from the feet it sees, ours and each peer's last frame, so nothing goes on the wire); one squashed before Carlsten reaches it is off his list, and he goes after the nearest left, or, with none left for him, stops where he is, says his thanks in the log of whoever is near (`COPY.toml` `slurms.thanks`) instead of his curse, turns round and walks back in the way he came (`slurms.ts` `afterTrodden`; all of them gone before he leaves the counter, he never comes out), and Castle Entropy's moat has its own, dropping off the rim, the island's edge and the cranes' hooks to crawl the floor, lie a while and melt into the concrete (`moatslurms.ts`, `CONFIG.slurms.moat`). The valley's music, the Bull Valley Scaduscope theme, loops quietly under everything from the main menu on, and from Begin on the valley's sounds (so far the strikes, the flashlight, the burns and bursts, the spiders and the Caretaker, heard from where they happen) play over it; a Music and a Sounds slider in the main menu's Audio and on the pause overlay set how loud. Shop the Citgo: cigarettes and joints on the counter, medicine on the rack, and the drinks behind the glass doors of the coolers along the back but the liquor (Wild Turkey, Jim Beam, Grey Goose), which stands at room temperature on an open shelf beside them, 3 of everything a day and 20 Ice Mountain. Matthew Marx's white Chevy is parked by the spawn Citgo, Marx reading at the tailgate; he reads for five minutes, then crosses Lake Avenue to the field between the road and the corn maze and does donuts for five, then drives home and reads again, over and over, and anyone arriving in the valley while he is at his donuts brings him straight home. Whenever the truck rolls, Day of Justice plays out of the cab (`truckradio.ts`, `CONFIG.truck.radio`, at the Music setting): as cut in the bed, and out of it fading and losing its highs with the distance, so from the Citgo only the lows of his donuts carry. At his donuts his rear wheels throw clods of grass and dirt and cut ruts in the field that heal slowly while he reads (`donutwake.ts`, `CONFIG.truck.donuts.spray` and `ruts`). Climb into the bed (`E`) and a 60-second countdown starts for everyone, and the first one in gets the map (`citgomap.ts`, opened for a charter): a click anywhere picks where he goes (snapped to the nearest road), Esc leaves it to him, and `E` hops out. When the countdown runs out he takes whoever is in the bed there and lets them off, then drives home empty, or with no pick on the joyride out through the valley and home again, letting them off at the Citgo. With no one else in the valley no countdown shows: he leaves as soon as the pick is made. Hop out (`E`) anywhere. `Y` whistles for him from anywhere while his bed is empty: he drives to you, you climb in, and he drives you home. Find cabbages in the wilderness and in the shade under the dishes behind the spawn Citgo (`cabbages.ts` `placeDishCabbages`, `CONFIG.cabbage.underDishes`), and they go into your pack, under Loot. Pickups, the Citgo shelves and whatever lies dropped all come back with the day, at midnight Central. Tab opens the pack over the valley with the pointer free, the raider standing on a turntable beside it in their character and whatever they wear (`avatarview.ts`; the Flaming Halo too), on its Consumables tab (Loot, where berries and cabbages go, Key Items, and Materials sit beside it; `A`/`D` switch): hovering an item shows its card (a small page of the Book of Shadows: the item spinning, its words, and its stats off the table, `itemstats.ts`: its kind, what it heals, how far it moves each ward and what that ward wards against, how long it works, how many to one, its shelf price), `E` uses it, `1`–`9` puts it on the hotbar along the bottom of the screen, whose number keys use it from then on, and `X` drops one (the open pack or bottle, or one of anything else) a little ahead of you, Shift-`X` the whole stack. `1`–`9` with no item under the cursor empties that slot, and a kind the pack runs out of (the last one used, dropped or stowed) leaves the bar on its own. The mouse works the open pack too (`packhud.ts` `wireMouse`, `bindings.ts` `PACK_MOUSE`): a click pins an item's card so the keys act on it, a double-click uses it, a drag puts it on a hotbar slot (every slot shows while the pack is open; a slot dragged off the bar or right-clicked empties), a right-click opens a menu of everything it can do, and at the locker a Shift-click moves the whole stack. `E` takes up only what the raider can see: a pickup within reach, about their own level, inside `CONFIG.player.pickupCone` of where they look, with no wall between (`interactions.ts`, `walls.ts` `between`), the one nearest the middle of the view first; a shelf unit the same, so nothing sells through the back room's partition. Anyone can take a drop up with `E`, as with any pickup; drops lie there until the day turns. The raider's state of mind is four wards, shown in the upper left as a red tetrahedron turning on its point (`vitalshud.ts`, `wards.ts` `tetraView`), each corner glowing with its level and carrying its mark (`icons.ts`: a weed leaf for high, a pill for stimulated, a beer bottle for drunk, a blotter tab for tripping): high the joint (and a little off the psychedelics), stimulated cigarettes, the energy drinks and anything swallowed as a pill (Adderall, aspirin, ibuprofen, Benadryl), drunk beer and liquor (Four Loko does both, and water sobers a little), tripping LSD and mushrooms (eye drops bring high and tripping down); each fades on its own (`CONFIG.wards.fadePerSecond`). Each is a layer of armor against its own shadows (`wards.ts`, `CONFIG.wards`): high against the shadowmen, stimulated against the spiders and spiderlings, drunk against the Caretaker, tripping against the tunnel shades and the Warden, the matching corner cutting a touch by up to 60% and the strongest other by up to 10% more; a chip under the bar for every ward going shows its strength. High also reaches the flashlight's beam further and wider, and stimulated quickens the sprint (`buffs.ts`, `CONFIG.buffs`). Every buff going (each of the four wards, and the joint's perception) shows as a cell in a row over the hotbar (`buffbar.ts` `activeBuffs`, `buffhud.ts`), its left edge on the bar's first slot (or, with nothing on the bar, where the bar would be), its mark under a dark wedge that grows as it winds down and the time left under it. Anything smoked, drunk or swallowed can be passed instead (`G`: over an item in the open pack, or in the valley the last thing used) to a raider on foot within `CONFIG.buffs.passReach` in front of you: they take it in full, its dose and its heal, and the giver catches a share of the dose (`shareScale`) and XP for sharing. Anything smoked or drunk also blurs the view for a second as it goes down, and three of the wards work on the view for as long as they last, each at full strength from half its level up and thinning as it fades (`wardview.ts`, drawn by `trails.ts`, tuned in `CONFIG.wardView`): drunk trails it, each frame keeping most of the last so that anything that moves, the view included, smears behind itself; high pushes every colour further from grey, more vivid, with a little of the drunk's trails; stimulated sharpens every edge. Shadowmen cross the valley around every raider, the same ones for everyone; one that passes close rushes, and in reach it draws back, winds up (`CONFIG.shadowmen.windupSeconds`) and lunges, every shadow the same and for everyone to see; a lunge lands only if you are still in reach, so a step back makes it miss. Every raider has geometrie, their hit points: 1,000 at level 1 and 40 more a level (`health.ts` `maxGeometrie`, `CONFIG.health`; 4,920 at level 99), shown Dark Souls style as a chunky red bar beside the tetrahedron in the upper left, as long as the raider's whole (it grows with the level), a pale trail of what a touch took draining after the fill, the points and the level on it. Each shadow's touch takes its own hit (`CONFIG.damage`: a spiderling 140, a shadowman 320, a tunnel shade 420, a spider 480, the Caretaker 750, the Warden 1,100, each give or take 10% on the valley's roll), cut by the wards (`wards.ts` `damageOf`); the number pops under the bar with what the wards took off it in the ward's colour (`hud.ts` `popDamage`, touches close together adding up), the view washes red and shakes, the first ward to take the edge off says so in the log, and the touch leaves you where you stand with a few seconds' grace (`CONFIG.health`). The touch that takes the last of it shatters your geometrie (GEOMETRIE SHATTERED over the static) and puts you back at the spawn Citgo, whole, with an empty pack but for its key items, which stay with you as Elden Ring's do: everything else you carried stays on your body where you fell (a corpse run). Standing on any Citgo forecourt gives geometrie back a little every couple of seconds until you are whole (`CONFIG.health.forecourt`: from nothing in a little under two minutes), so a heal from the pack is the quick way, and everything smoked, drunk or swallowed gives geometrie back as it moves the wards (`items.ts` `healsPct`, a share of whole: 15% for a cigarette, a drink, water or a pill, 30% for the joint, LSD, mushrooms, Four Loko and the liquor; never past whole). The locker comes through untouched, but the wallet comes open as the raider goes down: a share of it (`CONFIG.corpses.walletShare`, `corpses.ts` `spilledDimes`) lies in dimes where they fell, a drop like a burst's, for whoever gets there first, and the log says how much. Your body lies there, in the outfit you wore, your name floating over it as over a raider standing, for everyone to see (one at a time: die again before you reach it and it is gone, with everything on it), until you run back and take your things back with `E` over it; no one else can, and it lies through the day's turn. Behind the shelves of every Citgo a doorway at the back leads into a back room with a bank of lockers under an American flag, health and safety notices on the walls, and a bathroom walled off at its far end (a toilet, a sink under its mirror, EMPLOYEES MUST WASH HANDS, and a laptop on a card table against the wall, its screen the only light in the room, washing it blue, though a flashlight brought in lights it too, `bathglow.ts`); to the right of the lockers a steel back door stands open under an EXIT sign onto a concrete pad behind the store, a dumpster on it (`plazamodel.ts` `buildDumpster`, instanced with the store): `E` in front of them opens the pack with a Locker tab, your stash (the same at every Citgo, and no strike reaches it; a key item never goes in); `F` over an item stows one (the open pack or bottle, or one of anything else), Shift-`F` the stack, and on the Locker tab `F` and Shift-`F` take them back out. `E` at the laptop sits down at it (`laptopdialog.ts`): its screen fills the view, open on a text editor, white on dark blue, with the readme up (`LAPTOP.TXT`, always the first file, formatting kept as typed). Esc closes the editor onto an XP desktop, green hills under a blue sky with a taskbar and a start menu, and a window that browses `C:\Documents and Settings`, a folder for every raider: anyone's file opens to read, the raider's own to write and save (Ctrl+S), New Text Document makes another (up to `CONFIG.laptop.perAccount`), and Delete takes one of theirs away. Getting up (Esc on the desktop, or Turn Off Computer) leaves it for the next one on the editor and the readme again. The files are the valley's, the same at every Citgo. Every raider carries a flashlight in the left hand: the left button raises it and turns it on, and again puts it down; a shadowman held in anyone's beam for half a second bursts into black mist, for everyone, and leaves 1 to 8 dimes lying where it stood (`CONFIG.shadowmen.dimes`); anyone can take them up with `E`, like a drop, and they go into the wallet at 10¢ each, never the pack, with a line in the log. Marx's headlights burn them the same way, parked or driving: one held in them bursts, dimes and all, though no one is credited with it (`CONFIG.truck.headlights`). Among them come the shadow spiders: black spiders twice a shadowman's height on long legs arched high over their bodies, eight red eyes burning through the fog, coming up now and then anywhere and far more often near water (`CONFIG.shadowmen.spider`); one takes twice as long in a beam to burst, rushes a little faster, touches from further off, and leaves a $20 bill, cash the same way, and breaks into a brood of 3 to 8 spiderlings (`CONFIG.shadowmen.spiderling`): small, quick spiders that rush whoever is near from further off, touch for a block like any shadow, burst in a blink in a beam, and leave a few dimes each, never a tombstone, never counted toward the daily task or XP, never breaking again. A shadowman (and only a shadowman: no spider, spiderling, tunnel shade or the Warden) leaves a tombstone a step from its dimes, carved with the name it is given then (the Bull Valley Scaduscope's folklore names, carried over from forgotten-industries into `names.ts`), seen by everyone; the tombstones stay when the day turns, the oldest going past `CONFIG.graves.max`. An item used from the pack or the hotbar comes up in the right hand the same way, and goes down again. A trip (the tripping ward: LSD and mushrooms) shows what is not there, all of it in one psychedelic fuchsia (`fuchsia.ts`). Small platonic solids (tetrahedra, cubes, octahedra, dodecahedra, icosahedra) come and go a few metres round the eye, drifting, bobbing and tumbling slowly, faint-faced with solid edges, unlit and casting nothing, each fading in, staying a few seconds, fading out and coming again somewhere else; one more can show for every twelfth of the ward, so a full trip floats a dozen and the last of one only one or two (`tripsolids.ts`, `CONFIG.psych.solids`). Overhead, every so often a few stars near each other are joined by fuchsia lines into a constellation of their own, the stars lit fuchsia too, that fades in, holds and fades out; the further the trip the more often, and up to five at once (`constellations.ts`, `CONFIG.psych.constellations`). Both are the raider's own, seeded and never sent, and both thin out with the ward.

Tripping, the valley shows you people and things that are not there (`hallucinations.ts`, `hallucinationrig.ts`). Now and then while the tripping ward is up, more often and more at once the further in (from one every 40 seconds or so at a faint trip to one every 7 and four standing at once at a full one, `CONFIG.hallucinations`), someone or something flickers in out ahead of you on the ground: a raider in some character of the roster, standing or waving or dancing, under your own name or the name of someone else in the valley; a copy of Marx reading, Carlsten, Gron under his cloud, Erwin or Tyler; or a pickup lying in its halo. The people turn to watch you. They look real, and they answer to E as the real ones would: the glow rings them, a thing floats its label. E on one bursts it in psychedelic fuchsia (`fuchsia.ts`, the colour of everything a trip shows that is not there), a flash and a spray of small Platonic solids flung out, tumbling and fading, with a pitched-up shadow burst and a dry line in the log (`COPY.toml` `[hallucinations]`). Each stands half a minute or so and flickers out, and all of them go when the trip does or once you walk far from them. They are yours alone: nothing about them is sent or kept, no other raider sees them, and they never block your way.
